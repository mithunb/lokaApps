The standard pin

One marker for every place, on every atlas. This page settles its exact colour, outline, size and shadow by measuring them against the two grounds the product really draws on — the street map’s own painted colours and real satellite tiles over Bengaluru and Deoria — and shows every awkward case at true size. Nothing on this page is wired to the product.

The whole family at once: the standard pin every contributed place and every deoria village wears, and the two declared pins deoria keeps — sienna with its factory, moss with its flask. Same body, same white keyline, same shadow; only fill and heart differ.

  • Body20 × 28 px teardrop — a 17px round head, tip at the bottom middle. Width stays today’s 20, so everything that measures pins by width (folding distances, the key-row maths) does not move. measured
  • Colourrust-deep #7C3616 — already a product token (--color-rust-deep). Why this one is the next section. measured
  • Keyline2px white, unbroken round the whole body. On the pale street map it all but vanishes (1.1:1 on plain ground) and the pin reads as a clean dark drop; on satellite it snaps on at 4–19:1. measured
  • Heart12px white icon when the atlas declares one (deoria’s factory and flask, exactly as declared, drawn white); a white dot (3px radius) otherwise. The heart keeps the pin readable even where ground and fill match. drawn
  • Shadowdrop-shadow 0 1px 2px rgba(42,36,26,.45); the hover lift swaps to 0 3px 6px at .5, as today’s pins do. It follows the teardrop’s own edge, not a square box. drawn
  • SitsTip exactly on the place. The marker is already anchored bottom (atlas.js:1040), so the tip lands on the spot with no repositioning. One new line: the hover growth must grow from the tip — transform-origin:50% 100%. measured

What carries the visibility, plainly: on the street map, the fill — 8.0:1 on plain ground, 6.8:1 on parks, 5.4:1 on water, and 3.1:1 on its worst ground, the map’s tree-green, right at the 3:1 line where a mark stays readable measured. On satellite, the white parts — keyline and heart run 4–19:1 over vegetation and water, exactly where every fill fails (≤2.1:1) measured; and on the brightest rooftops, where white fails (1.2:1), the fill takes over again at 7.1:1 measured. The shadow exists for the one ground where both sit near the line — that same tree-green — where it rims the white with dark drawn below.

The verdict the coordinator asked for, plainly: moss cannot hold on satellite by its fill — nowhere, not just over vegetation measured. It does not need to. The white keyline wraps the whole silhouette at 8–18:1 there, the white flask fills the head, and the shadow covers the street map’s tree-green. No heavier keyline, no darker moss, no size change needed — the white parts, not the fill, are what a dark ground sees. measured for the numbers, drawn above for the eye to agree.

  • The name is missing, or is just the file’s own name (his real second upload is labelled df_blr beside a file called user-df-blr.geojson — the same words once the user- front and the dashes are ignored): name the person and the day instead, from the upload’s own record.
    Source · Upload by Priya Kumar, 13 Aug 2026
  • No name and nobody recorded: just the day.
    Source · Upload from 13 Aug 2026

Never the email address — it identifies, it does not introduce. Person and day come from addedBy and addedAt, which every upload already carries measured.

One upload or several? The line reads the same either way, and it always shows: one pin belongs to one upload, so the row simply names this pin’s upload. When an atlas has several uploads the row is doing the ring’s old work; when it has one, it is a quiet credit — and a popup shared out of an atlas still says where its place came from. Only uploads get the row: deoria’s curated layers keep their credits panel, their popups unchanged. drawn

What changes, what stays — the implementation map

Six changes, all small; and a list of things that must not move, so they can be checked after. Line numbers are today’s measured.

Changes

  • The pin element (atlas.js addMarker ≈1028, and keyPinEl 1271 — one shared builder): the 20px circle becomes the 20×28 teardrop. Fill: the stanza’s declared per-kind colour if it has one, else rust-deep. Heart: the declared icon drawn white, else the white dot. ring and glyph stop being read.
  • One colour (oneColorOf, atlas.js 1262): returns #7C3616 for every contributed layer — the per-upload pick and the rust fallback go.
  • CSS (index.html ≈365): .atlas-pin becomes the teardrop block — 20×28, filter:drop-shadow(0 1px 2px rgba(42,36,26,.45)), transform-origin:50% 100%; hover swaps to 0 3px 6px at .5. .atlas-rowed height 20 → 28; everything else in that block keeps its numbers.
  • Popup clearances (atlas.js 3413–3421): above-the-head −26 → −34; beside-the-waist −12 → −20 (the head’s middle is 18px up, was 10); below-the-foot +6 stays. And the ordinary un-fanned popup, which today opens with no offset at all — on the pin — should get offset 30 so the bubble clears the head.
  • The Source line (popupHTML, atlas.js 3444): for userLayer layers append the last row per the wording above (label → person+day → day).
  • The panel’s own dot: the layer list’s little colour dot reads the same colour the pin wears (through oneColorOf), so a shelf row can never disagree with its pins.

Stays — check these after

  • Anchor bottom (1040): untouched — the tip lands on the place by construction.
  • Width 20 (PIN_W, 1292): folding distances, fan spacing and the flip maths all measure width — none of them move.
  • Key marks: shapes, sizes, 2px gaps, left/right:23px; top:10px — all unchanged.
  • Group discs (1841): colours, sizes, white ring, white count — untouched.
  • Deoria’s manifest and files: byte-identical; its colours and icons are read as declared. Villages’ own fold badge (“Cluster 1” chip) untouched.
  • The name under the pin (.atlas-mlabel): still hangs under the marker — now under the tip, which is under the place.
  • Worth one look, not measured: the fan ring’s radius was tuned around 20px circles (SPIDERFY); with 28px pins a fanned pair may sit closer than before. Open the fan once after implementing and judge.

Open questions that are Mithun’s, not mine: (1) The wizard still offers five marker colours per upload — with one standard colour that pick now changes nothing on the map. Retire it for markers, or keep it for lines and shapes only? (2) If reach should ever beat identity, ink is the measured runner-up. (3) The moss-pin/moss-disc kinship above — acceptable as shipped?
