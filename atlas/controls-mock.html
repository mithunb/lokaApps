Where the map's controls should live

The floating panel currently holds three ranks of thing at once: controls that belong to the whole atlas (Map/Satellite, which region it covers), the list of layers, and each layer's own detail (keys, labels, credits). Search has already left the panel and floats alone over the middle of the map. This page tests the obvious tidy-up — move everything into a horizontal header — against both real atlases, and shows where it breaks and what works instead.

The split that survives contact with both atlases: a slim strip across the top of the map for what is true of the whole atlas — Map/Satellite, search, and (for the owner) the region — and the panel keeps only the layers and their keys. Keys are lists, and lists need a column: Deoria's seventeen switches would need two and a half of these screens laid end to end.

1 · Try it — two atlases, two arrangements

Everything drawn on the stage uses the product's own colours, type and controls. Proposed makes two changes at once: the strip, and panel rows redrawn with the same row discipline these study pages use — names that lead, counts in ink, one skeleton per row, and groups separated three times as far as the rows inside them.

2 · The honest width test — why the keys can't ride a header

Here are Deoria's real seventeen layer switches laid end to end, at the exact size they render in the product. The red marker is the full width of the stage above. Scroll the bar to feel what a reader would have to do.

stage width

And switches are the small half of it. A key is a list — the crops key alone is eleven lines (nine crops and two greys) — and a list read sideways stops being a key. The things that do fit a header are the ones that are one answer each: which base map, one search box, which region. That is the whole strip.

3 · What moves, what stays

Three pieces move up; everything that lists moves nothing. The panel's first scroll-stop becomes a layer, not chrome.

Up to the strip

  • Map / Satellite — one answer for the whole map.
  • Search — already floats over the map today; the strip gives it a home instead of mid-air.
  • “Covers Bengaluru Urban · Change” — the region row, owner-only, with its note.

The strip costs no map that isn't already spent: the search box floats in this band now, and the panel starts a full region-row later. On phones the strip carries search only; the bottom sheet stays exactly as it is.

Staying in the panel

  • Layer switches, in their groups — with a count on every group head, so a closed group still says how much it holds.
  • Keys and their kinds — lists, under the layer they belong to, behind the moss rule.
  • “Browse all 335 labels”, per-layer Change, and the credit lines.

The panel's job sharpens: it is the shelf of layers, nothing else. Seventeen switches with keys need about two window-heights of column — exactly what a panel has and a header never will.

What this page recommends. Not a header instead of the panel, and not the panel as it is: a strip for the atlas, a shelf for its layers. The strip is one new element plus three relocations, all of them pieces that give one answer each. The panel change is a redraw, not a rethink — same switches, same keys, same order, drawn with the row discipline of these study pages (the name leads its row, counts sit in ink, one skeleton repeats, groups breathe three times wider than rows). Tested here against the smallest atlas and the heaviest one. Nothing on this page touches the live product.
