More than two keys on one layer — what it looks like, at real density

A design mock, not the product. Every place below is a real place from the Bengaluru layer (66 places), with its real kinds for categories, themes and creator. The fourth key, state of repair, is invented for this mock, because the data has only three columns that qualify.

The rule you are judging: colours repeat between keys; the shape of a mark says which key it belongs to. Circles are the first key. Small solid marks — square, triangle, diamond — are the second, third and fourth.

The map at density

Centred on the densest neighbourhood (14 real places within 400 m; “as it grows” doubles that to ~30, which is where this layer is heading). Pins that would genuinely overlap fold into a counted disc — the same rule the live map uses, with the fold distance matching the marks’ true width. Streets are sketched in; the pins, sizes and distances are exact.

One place, up close

The same place carrying three keys and four keys, in both arrangements — at true size, then enlarged three times so you can see what the reader is being asked to read. In a row, every mark stays 20 px and keeps trying to hold an icon: the diamond’s icon shrinks to 8 px and the triangle cannot hold one at all. Around the pin, the first key keeps its full 20 px circle and icon, and the other keys become small solid marks whose colour and shape carry everything.

Which shapes actually survive

Measured, not guessed: each pair of shapes was drawn at true size and compared pixel-for-pixel, calibrated against the one pair already shipped and approved — circle vs square at 20 px. A pair counts as “reads apart” only if it differs at least as much as that pair does. Verdicts below hold from 12 px up.

Phone width

The same neighbourhood on a 375 px screen, three keys on. A row of three 20 px marks is 64 px — a sixth of the screen’s width per place. Around the pin it is 40 px — a tenth.

The cost that was accepted

Colours repeat between keys. Below, the same wine colour on a circle (a category) and on a small square (a theme), side by side at true size. A reader who ignores shape will read these as the same thing. That trade has been accepted — the key panel says it in words, and the shape is the tell.
