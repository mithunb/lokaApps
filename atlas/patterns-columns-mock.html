One paid reading of 66 places costs three model calls. Whether it earns its money is decided before it starts: by which columns it is fed. Today there is no decision at all: every written column is glued together and sent, ids and street addresses included (see the specimen below). The marked stretches are paid for but say nothing about what the place is: the id word, who added it (the same name on 59 of 66 places), and the address — whose commas make "Bengaluru", "Karnataka 560086" and "India" count as label words on every single place. The reading still finds patterns despite this, because the model shrugs off chaff. But it is money and attention spent on noise, and on a bigger layer the chaff crowds out the signal.

B3 · Counting goes first, free — reading is offered only where counting cannot reach

Before any money, three things are counted per column, instantly: how many places have it filled in, how much its words repeat across places, and whether those repeats split the map into a few groups or one big pile. Six columns counting rules out: no reading will be offered for these
  • address — 7 shared addresses gather 19 places, but by street, and the biggest pile is 4. Enough to search, too little to colour a map.
  • creator — 5 names, one of them on 59 of 66 places: one big pile, not a split.

  • id — 66 words, all different by design. Nothing can ever repeat.
