Fork C — one list, or two doors

Patterns arrive two ways: counting a column (free, instant, checkable by tapping) and reading one with AI (paid, slower, a judgement). The fork is whether the owner meets them in one list with the same two choices per row — make it a key, or leave it as words to browse — or through two separate doors, as today. The honest reason they might stay apart is money, so the cost is drawn on every row and every door.

I would ship C1 — one list for the owner. Every row ends at the same two choices, so making the owner learn two doors teaches him the plumbing, not the choice. The price difference is carried by a badge on the row — free says counted, paid says read, and when, which is more honest than carrying it by architecture, because a door's name cannot say what this row cost.
What survives of the second door Browsing stays its own door, for readers. "Browse all 335 labels" is not a decision surface; it is the words themselves, open to everyone. C1 replaces only the owner's side. Nothing a reader sees today changes under either choice.
