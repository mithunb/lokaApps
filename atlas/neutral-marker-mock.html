The place pin goes neutral

Your ask, verbatim: “Let the marker for a place be a neutral graphic. The shapes and color of the keys — don’t they suffice. What information is lost by making the place marker neutral?” This page hunts every loss we could find, draws the neutral graphic at true size on both grounds, and draws the one real cost honestly so you can judge it.

Context already settled: the colour control for places is leaving (you approved that reading). “Colour places by” is the owner’s question on the layer screens — for pins it really asks which column becomes a key, and the pin itself wears one colour per layer: a 2 px ring. The question left is exactly yours — does that ring earn its keep?

I would ship A neutral pin for every contributed place, on every atlas: the white 20 px body LOKA already draws, ring fixed in rust, meaning nothing. Keys keep carrying the kinds. Nothing readers reliably have today is lost — the ring’s one job, telling uploads apart, is a job it already fails at (drawn in §4).
The one real loss — and the mend Two uploads on, no key on: pins stop saying which upload a place came from. Mend it where meaning already lives: the popup names the upload, and “Show key” offers a ready-made key — which upload — whenever an atlas carries more than one. It answers with names, marks and counts; the ring answered with 2 px of colour nobody was told to read — and in the only three-upload atlas, two uploads wear the same rust anyway.

1 · Where a layer’s colour can reach a reader — all of it

The sweep covered every reader-facing surface: pins, group discs, the fan, the hover bubble, the popup, the layer shelf, the key panel, sharing. The layer’s colour lives on exactly two surfaces, and the second only exists while no key is on. There is no printed or exported picture anywhere in the product — the map is the map. All measured; lines in the footer.

The pin carries it

White 20 px body, 2 px ring in the layer’s one colour. The ring is the layer colour’s entire effect on the map. Left: today’s default rust. Right: the same pin wearing a key — the rows beside it carry the kinds; the ring still only says “layer”.

The group disc never did

Pins that collide fold into a moss disc — moss for every layer, deeper with count. And the population that folds is all visible layers together: one disc can hold places from several uploads. Groups already answer “which upload?” with silence.

The hover bubble never did

Names the place; with keys on it repeats the kind rows in miniature — the little mark that names the key is a deliberate grey, claiming no colour. No layer colour, no upload name.

The popup never did

Title, kinds, the details the owner chose. The upload’s name is never in it. A reader who clicks to find out which upload a place came from learns nothing — today the ring is the only sighted path, and nothing explains the ring.

The layer shelf small print

The row itself is colourless — switch and name. While no key is on, one small dot under the row wears the layer’s colour: colour’s second and last home. Drawn here as the real three-upload atlas has it: both dots the same rust.

3 · The neutral graphic, drawn at true size

Neutral means: one fixed look, no control changes it, no reader must decode it. It does not have to mean grey. Four candidates, on the pale ground and on satellite-dark — each alone, wearing a key, and beside the disc it folds into. Streets are drawn stand-ins; sizes are the live map’s exact geometry.

On the pale map (the default ground)
On satellite (the dark ground)
The anatomy, at 6×. The body stays 20 px — it is the finger target, and the fold rules measure the map in units of it (pins fold when closer than one pin). The body stays white — on satellite the white disc is what survives, not the ring. The ring stays 2 px — any thinner vanishes on satellite, any thicker crowds the key marks beside it. The shadow and the small hover lift are the live pin’s own. Nothing about the graphic changes with the layer, the atlas, or anyone’s choice — that is the whole point.
The graphic I would ship — A, the rust ring LOKA already draws Declare today’s default pin the pin: white body, rust ring, for every contributed place everywhere. The change is in meaning, not in looks — rust stops being “this layer’s colour” and becomes “a place someone marked”, the way every road sign in a country wears one red. Most atlases will not change by a pixel on the day this ships. Rust sits nowhere near the five kind colours the keys use, so a ring never argues with a mark beside it.
Not B (ink). The heaviest thing on the map, everywhere. Ink is what words wear — sixty ink rings compete with every street name.
Not C (moss). Moss already means two things — “a group of places” on the discs and “switched on” in the panel. And it sits one hue family from the green a kind can wear (●) — at 2 px that is an argument.
Not D (a solid dot). Loses the white body that satellite needs, shrinks the finger target, and 20 px is the unit the fold geometry is built on.

Scope: hand-set markers in curated atlases (Deoria’s factory icons, its solid village pins) are their own decision and keep their meaning. This page is about the pin contributed places wear.

The two live-shaped uploads of the three-upload test atlas: LOKA Places · Bengaluru (66 places) and Grown neighbourhood (density test) (132 places), drawn at street zoom over Halasuru with the live fold rules (pins fold when closer than one pin; discs at the live sizes and mosses). The grown upload revisits every finds spot centimetres apart — pairs that close fold at any zoom, so the worlds draw the finds plus the grown upload’s 66 genuinely new spots, and one revisited pair is fanned open below. World 1 — today, if the owner had chosen well (rust vs slate)

Readable — if a reader notices the small dots under the switches and thinks to match ring hues across a map. Nothing in the product teaches this, the popup never confirms it, and every disc has already erased it. This is the best case the ring can offer.

The same spot, mended

Neutral pins, which-upload key on: each fanned pin wears its upload’s mark, and clicking either names it in words. The fan finally answers the question stacking asks.

Also weighed, and set aside

Colour returns only while two uploads are on
The same pin means different things on different days — a reader cannot learn it. And it returns wearing the same five-colour wardrobe that already failed: two uploads can still both be rust.
A different pin shape per upload
Shape already means kind in the rows beside the pin. Two shape languages a finger’s width apart is the split-body lesson from the marker page all over again.
Key marks wear the layer’s colour
One mark, one kind, one colour is the rows’ whole grammar — bending it for uploads breaks reading everywhere else on the map.

Open for you: the ready-made key’s name. The drawing says “which upload”; “added as”, “whose places”, or a wording of yours would drop in without changing the design. And whether the popup’s “from the upload …” line should appear even on one-upload atlases (the drawing shows it only when there are two or more).
